How prices move

  1. 1

    Every robot starts at $100

    Whoever lists it, and whatever its backtest says, a robot goes on sale at $100. Its author's claims are shown next to it, but the price only listens to what it does from then on.

  2. 2

    Every trade goes on record

    From the day it is listed, each trade it takes is read from the broker account it runs on, with real costs: spread, commission and overnight swap. No editing, no deleting. Losing trades stay.

  3. 3

    The price follows the record

    Every Monday the price is reset: the listing price times what its record has grown since listing. If it loses, it gets cheaper.

Questions

How is the record kept?

Each robot trades on a broker account, demo or real, that we read every week with read-only access. Its record is the trades that account closes, with their real costs. Nobody can edit or delete a trade, its author included.

Why can't a bigger bet make a bigger record?

Every trade is measured at the same risk: the size at which its author's own backtest would have fallen 20% at worst. Ten lots or one, the record is the same. A backtest that hides its falls makes its robot fall sooner here.

Is a rising price a promise?

No. It tells you what the robot has done since it went on sale, not what it will do. Good records end too.

What do I get when I buy?

The robot for the platform named on its page, a setup guide, and its updates: your order link always downloads the latest version. Its public record keeps running.

Where do the robots come from?

From traders who list the robots they trust, and from our own lab. Ours go on the board only after passing our own tests and trading live.
